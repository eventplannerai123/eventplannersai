# What the clause actually does at 60 days

The clause is **built, not real** — written to be one-sided in the ways venue
contracts genuinely are, the same way the Sep 23 catering invoice was built with
deliberate errors. Every mechanism in it appears in real contracts; what is
artificial is having all of them in one place.

On a **$85,000** Estimated Total:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Deposit paid at signing (25%) | $21,250 — non-refundable, **not credited** |
| Liquidated damages at 60 days (75%) | $63,750 |
| **Total cost of cancelling** | **$85,000** |

**Cancelling 60 days out costs exactly what holding the event would have cost.**
That is the payoff, and it falls out of 9.1 plus 9.2 rather than being stated
anywhere.

## The four things a careful read catches

1. **9.1 — the deposit is not credited.** Most contracts apply the deposit
   against the cancellation fee. This one keeps it and charges the fee on top,
   so the bands are 25 points higher than they look.
2. **9.3 — "Estimated Total" is not what you would have spent.** It includes the
   22% service charge and tax on a Food and Beverage Minimum **whether or not
   incurred**. You pay service charge on food nobody ate and tax on a bill
   nobody ran up.
3. **9.2 — the 60-day boundary is a cliff, not a slope.** 60 days is 75%, 59
   days is 100%. One day is worth 25% of the Estimated Total — $21,250 on an
   $85,000 event.
4. **9.4 — the cliff is a day earlier than it looks.** Notice counts on receipt,
   and anything after 5pm lands the next business day. Email at 5:30pm on a
   Friday at 61 days and you are inside the 59-day band by Monday.

9.5 looks like relief and mostly is not: it returns half the damages as credit,
only at the same venue, only within twelve months, and it evaporates if the
rebooked event moves. 9.6 is the one to read twice — it explicitly excludes
reduced attendance and loss of funding, which are the two reasons a corporate
event actually gets cancelled.
