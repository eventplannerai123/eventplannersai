# What time to post — what the research actually says

Written 2026-10-15, because she asked twice and got a shrug the first time.
Her brief: "dont necessarily look at my numbers. do some reasearch across the
internet. we are still a new account and dont know whats best."

**All times in this file are Eastern.**

## The published studies contradict each other

This is the finding, not a failure to find one. Five large analyses, five
different answers for Reels:

| Source | Sample | Says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Later](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) | 6M+ posts, ~975k Reels | **Midnight–6am**, best at 5am; Reels peak midnight Monday |
| [SocialPilot](https://www.demandsage.com/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) | 250k Reels | **Mon–Thu 8am–noon** |
| [Hopper HQ](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) | undisclosed | **Tue/Wed 7–9am** |
| [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/) | undisclosed | **Midweek afternoons**, Tue 1–7pm, Wed noon–9pm |
| [RivalIQ](https://createsocial.ai/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-reels) | undisclosed | **Overnight 10pm–7am**, with a Thu/Fri/Sun 7–10pm peak |

Midnight against midday against mid-afternoon. They cannot all be right, and
the spread is bigger than any effect they claim to measure. The likeliest
explanation is that each tool is reporting **its own customers' audiences**,
which differ.

**So do not treat any single published time as an instruction.** The one thing
every source agrees on is that your own audience beats the averages.

## What survives across all of them

- **Midweek beats weekends.** Every source ranks Sat/Sun lowest.
- **Tue–Thu is the strongest block.**
- For a **B2B / working-professional audience** — which event planners are —
  the most repeated window is **Tue–Thu, 10am–noon**, with Wednesday strongest
  ([eclincher](https://www.eclincher.com/articles/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin-for-b2b-financial-services-2026)).
  Evening (3–6pm, or later) shows up as a contested, test-it option rather than
  a default.
- Commute-hour posting has evidence only for **news and media** audiences, not
  B2B.

Most of these are vendor blogs rather than controlled studies, so treat the
exact hours as directional.


**61% of everything went out between 9 and 11am**, and only two posts in the
account's history have ever gone out after 3pm. So neither her numbers nor
anyone's can tell her whether a different time is better — she has no variation
to measure.

That also means the morning habit is not wrong. **9–11am is almost exactly the
window the B2B sources recommend**, which is the one place the research and her
practice already agree.

## What to do

1. **Keep mornings as the default.** It matches the only consensus there is for
   this audience, and changing it on the strength of contradictory blog posts
   would be worse than leaving it.
2. **The Oct 15 evening second post (6:30pm) is the first real test of another
   window** — the first after-3pm post since the account started, other than
   one. Keep it, and note what it does.
3. **If she wants a real answer**, it has to be deliberate: alternate ~10am and
   ~6:30pm for a fortnight and compare per-post reach.

**Honest caveat, and it is the same one that sank the cadence test**
([`OCT-CADENCE-TEST.md`](OCT-CADENCE-TEST.md)): reach on this account is small
and has been falling, so a two-week time test will be noisy and may not
resolve. Pre-register what counts as a difference before starting, or it will
be read the way the length test was — as a result, when it was noise.
