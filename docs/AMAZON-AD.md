# Amazon Sponsored Products — "Calm Before the Aisle - SP test"

Campaign `A09999666H7PSCLNRKUE`, ad group `A055838184AJK2FMU536`. $3.30/day,
$50 total cap, extended to Mon 30 Nov (Sahiba, Nov 16). **amazon.com and the
ad console are blocked from this session**, so every figure here is read off
a screenshot she sends.

| Week | Impressions | Clicks | Sales | Purchases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nov 9–15 (7 days) | 772 | 4 | $11.99 | 1 |
| Nov 16–21 (6 days) | **377** | **2** | **$0.00** | **0** |

Read from her console on Sat 21 Nov. Per day that is 110 impressions falling
to 63, so down about 43% rather than the 51% the raw totals suggest — the
second window is a day shorter.

**The one sale is still the only sale.** It landed in the Nov 9–15 window on
"wedding coloring book", the keyword whose bid was raised to $0.65. Nothing
has converted since.

**Spend is the number that puts the rest in proportion.** A chart covering a
range that included the sale showed total cost $2.30 against $11.99 of sales,
ROAS 5.21. Against a $3.30 *daily* budget over nearly two weeks, that is the
campaign barely delivering at all: roughly $2–3 spent of a $50 cap. The
return on what it did spend is fine; there is just almost nothing of it.

So the question for the recap is not "is the ROAS good" — it is whether a
campaign that cannot spend $3.30 a day is worth leaving on until Nov 30, or
whether the bid is the thing holding it back.
